Research areas

JCAS (Joint Communications and Sensing)

I developed RF system designs and signal-processing methods for integrated sensing and communication (ISAC), enabling wireless systems to communicate and sense using shared spectrum and hardware resources. The resulting work supports applications such as UAV detection, environmental monitoring, and traffic safety, contributing to practical sensing capabilities for sixth-generation (6G) wireless systems.

Neural-network-based wireless signal processing

I developed learning-based models to address practical wireless-system challenges. Specifically, neural-network-based digital predistortion reduces transmitter nonlinearity while preserving waveform quality, and blind source separation enables channel equalization under non-ideal channel conditions. These results demonstrate how AI can improve the linearity, reliability, and efficiency of wireless hardware.

Advanced 4-D millimeter-wave imaging & radar

I designed compressive reflector antennas and a software-defined MIMO radar architecture that capture informative measurements of moving objects without mechanical scanning. This approach addresses the speed limitations of conventional synthetic-array and phased-array security-screening systems, bringing millimeter-wave sensing closer to practical on-the-move security screening.

High-capacity, low-complexity wireless communication

I experimentally demonstrated simultaneous multiplexing and demultiplexing of at least four orbital-angular-momentum (OAM) modes using a pair of identical OAM antennas. This mode-division-multiplexing approach increases wireless capacity while retaining a simple receiver architecture.

Antennas and circuits for practical electromagnetic applications

I developed antenna and RF-circuit solutions and validated them through end-to-end experiments, such as orthogonal beamforming, single-frequency imaging, and quantitative dielectric-material characterization.
