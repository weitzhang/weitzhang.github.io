Research areas

JCAS (Joint Communications and Sensing)

This research develops RF design and signal-processing methods that allow wireless systems to communicate and sense simultaneously.

Learning-based methods address key challenges in practical JCAS systems: digital predistortion mitigates transmitter nonlinearity while preserving waveform quality, and blind source separation supports channel equalization under non-ideal channel conditions. Together, these approaches advance efficient 6G hardware and reliable JCAS performance.

Advanced 4-D millimeter-wave imaging & radar

This research combines compressive reflector antennas with software-defined MIMO radar to capture informative measurements of moving objects without mechanical scanning.

Fast FMCW waveforms support high-throughput 3-D imaging with velocity estimation and motion compensation, bringing millimeter-wave sensing closer to practical on-the-move security screening.

High-capacity low-complexity wireless communication

This work uses orbital-angular-momentum mode division multiplexing to increase wireless capacity while keeping receiver architectures simple.

Experiments with multiple orthogonal modes demonstrate how electromagnetic structure and signal processing can work together for efficient, high-capacity links.

Antennas, circuits for practical electromagnetic applications

These projects are grounded in hardware: antennas, RF circuits, and end-to-end experiments for practical electromagnetic applications.

One example uses reconfigurable reflectarrays and controlled electromagnetic modes for single-frequency imaging and quantitative dielectric-material characterization. A second example uses Cassegrain reflector antennas in a 4×4 OAM-MDM system to multiplex and demultiplex four orthogonal modes at 10 GHz.
